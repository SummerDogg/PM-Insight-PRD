# Product Insight & PRD: Create a PRD

Create a concise, evidence-aware Product Requirements Document (PRD) that aligns stakeholders and guides design and engineering work.

## Workflow

1. Read the user's brief and any attached research or product documents. Use those materials first.
2. Identify the problem, target users, desired outcome, success measures, constraints, and scope.
3. Ask focused questions only when missing information would materially change the PRD. Mark unanswered items as assumptions or open questions.
4. Use relevant user or market research when requested or needed to resolve a material gap. Keep evidence, estimates, inferences, and assumptions distinct; never invent research findings.
5. Draft the PRD using the eight-section structure below.
6. Save a substantial PRD as `PRD-[product-name].md` in the user's workspace unless they request another format or location.

## PRD structure

Use these sections in order:

1. **Executive Summary** — What the product or feature is, whom it serves, and why now.
2. **Problem & Background** — The user problem, current situation, prior research, and relevant context.
3. **Objectives & Success Metrics** — Goals, explicit non-goals, baselines, targets, and how each metric will be measured.
4. **Target Users & Segments** — The intended users, their jobs and needs, key contexts, and constraints.
5. **Value Proposition & Requirements** — The value users should receive, prioritized user stories, and observable acceptance criteria. Group requirements by P0, P1, and P2 when that helps scope the release.
6. **Solution Overview** — The high-level approach, key features, design decisions, and technical considerations when known.
7. **Open Questions & Assumptions** — Unresolved questions, owners where known, and hypotheses that need validation.
8. **Release Plan & Phasing** — First-release scope, later phases, dependencies, and relative timing. Avoid invented dates.

## Quality bar

- Make the problem, target user, outcome, and scope easy to find.
- Prefer specific, measurable success criteria over vague goals.
- Separate non-goals from requirements to prevent scope creep.
- Tie claims to supplied evidence or identified research. Label estimates and assumptions.
- Keep the PRD concise and use plain, accessible language.
